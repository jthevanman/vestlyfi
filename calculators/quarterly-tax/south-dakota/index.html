How quarterly taxes work in South Dakota

Because South Dakota does not tax personal income, the only quarterly estimated taxes you owe as a self-employed resident are federal. That is still two pieces: self-employment tax — 15.3% for Social Security and Medicare on 92.35% of your net profit, up to the Social Security wage base — and federal income tax on your profit after the standard deduction.

The IRS asks you to pay these in four installments, generally due April 15, June 15, September 15, and the following January 15. There is no South Dakota estimated-tax return to file.

You avoid an IRS underpayment penalty by meeting a "safe harbor": paying at least 90% of this year's tax, or 100% of last year's tax (110% if your income is higher). You can pay online at IRS Direct Pay or through EFTPS. Since there is no South Dakota return to file, the calculator above shows your federal quarterly amounts only. You can pay online at the Internal Revenue Service (federal only) (payment portal).

South Dakota Estimated Tax FAQ

Do I have to pay quarterly estimated taxes in South Dakota?
You still owe federal quarterly estimated taxes on self-employment income, but South Dakota has no state income tax, so there is no separate state estimate to file. Your quarterly payments go to the IRS only.
When are 2026 estimated taxes due?
Federal estimated payments for 2026 are due April 15, June 15, September 15, and January 15 of the following year. Federal estimated payments are due April 15, June 15, September 15, and the following January 15.
How much should I set aside for taxes as a 1099 worker in South Dakota?
A common rule of thumb is 25–30% of your net self-employment income — and in South Dakota you can stay near the lower end since there is no state income tax. The calculator above gives you a far more precise number based on your actual income and filing status.
What is unique about estimated taxes in South Dakota?
South Dakota has no state income tax, so self-employed residents only owe federal quarterly estimates.
Are there other South Dakota-specific rules I should know?
There is no South Dakota estimated-tax return to file.
Does this calculator include the QBI deduction?
Not in this version. The Qualified Business Income (QBI) deduction can reduce your federal taxable income by up to 20% of qualifying business profit, so your real federal tax may be a little lower than shown. We keep the estimate conservative and leave QBI out; factor it in with a tax professional if it applies to you.
